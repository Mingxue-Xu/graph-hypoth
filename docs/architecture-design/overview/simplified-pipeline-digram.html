Claim Graph — The Complete Pipeline

A multi-agent pipeline comprising seven specialized agent roles, together with deterministic procedures and validation gates, that constructs a claim graph from a researcher's seed claim or research goal. One role — the Critic Panel — is instantiated by two independent critic agents plus a conditionally consulted third judge. Section 1 gives the complete pipeline; section 2 shows the same pipeline simplified, in plain words.

1 · The complete pipeline

The pipeline comprises seven specialized agent roles together with deterministic procedures and validation gates, in runtime order: extraction → evidence verification (every edge with retrieved evidence) → priority annotation → concept mining → hypothesis proposal ⟷ Critic Panel → panel aggregation → revision → panel → aggregation → acceptance gate and ranking → researcher confirmation → experiment design ⟷ experiment review → export.

The claim graph is versioned: each accepted transaction produces a new graph revision. Hypothesis generation follows a generate–critique–revise loop: propose → panel → revise → panel — two panel rounds and one revision round; the panel's two critic agents act as the judges, and a third judge is consulted only when the two judges disagree.

Evidence verification is exhaustive and up-front: every accepted edge with retrieved evidence passages is verified before hypothesis generation begins, rather than on demand; hypothesis edges are not verified at this stage (they carry no retrieved evidence passages — their grounding is deferred to the experiment stage). In evidence aggregation, per-passage signals — together with the open risks and qualifiers the reviewer flags across the passages — are fused deterministically into a single edge-level verdict; a verdict is accepted only when its confidence clears a 0.5 threshold, otherwise the edge is recorded as insufficient; the two best verdicts within δ = 0.025 of each other count as a tie and also yield insufficient; and a verdict whose evidence links lack a required evidence role is withheld from commit and recorded as an open risk, leaving the edge unverified. With the shipped configuration only the support role is scheduled, so an edge commits as supported or insufficient.

Each candidate hypothesis must pass a hypothesis acceptance gate — minimum bars on novelty, testability, scope fit, and duplication, applied before ranking; its quality score is computed deterministically from the proposer's self-reported signals and the panel's median novelty grade, and its ranking score is the quality score discounted by the panel's median saturation (quality × (1 − saturation)); the top-k ranked candidates are presented to the researcher or resolved by the profile's confirmation policy.

Terminology.
Claim graph: a directed graph in which nodes represent concepts and each directed edge records a relationship that a source asserts from source to target; edges carry their evidence links and, once verified, a status and confidence. Its form is borrowed from causal graphs, its semantics are not: an edge records what a source asserts, not what an intervention would show.
Agent: a large language model (LLM) instance assigned a specialized role in the pipeline and invoked with a fixed role prompt; only LLM roles are agents — transaction validation, scoring, aggregation, and export are deterministic procedures.
Graph transaction (Δ): a typed bundle of proposed graph changes (Δ-extract, Δ-verify, Δ-priority, Δ-hypothesis, Δ-experiment) that must pass five deterministic acceptance checks — well-formedness of the payload, referential integrity, currency of the base revision, legality of the status transition, and idempotence — before the graph is updated; each accepted transaction produces a new graph revision. Δ-extract covers both seed-derived and literature-mined additions; the latter appears in the diagram as Δ-extract (mined).
Verdict: the edge-level outcome the deterministic fusion selects — support, contradict, qualify, not causal, or insufficient — which becomes the edge status supported, contradicted, qualified, not_causal, or insufficient when committed.
Evidence role: the functional part an evidence passage plays for the edge under verification — support, contradiction, qualification, mechanism, or confounder; each verdict requires a matching role among the edge's evidence links, and the missing-role guard withholds a proposal whose links lack it, recording an open risk instead.
Mechanism chain: the ordered sequence of steps through which a hypothesis links its source concept to its target; candidate hypotheses state their chain explicitly and the panel grades each step.
Confounder: a concept that influences both the source and the target of an edge without lying on the mechanism chain between them.
Mediator: a concept that lies on that chain. Both are node types the experiment stage reads as controls.
Saturation: a penalty in [0, 1] reflecting how densely the retrieved literature already covers a candidate hypothesis's territory.
Borda rank fusion: rank aggregation by summing rank positions across judges.
Top-k: the k highest-ranked candidates presented to the researcher.

Input scoping: the seed from which the pipeline extracts is either a claim or a research goal — both are accepted as the seed input, and the pipeline does not branch on which it is. The seed arrives as part of a researcher profile — a structured input containing the seed claim or research goal together with a description of the researcher's expertise. Concepts are tagged by origin as seed-derived or literature-mined.

Two structural notes: (1) the Research Synthesist runs as a single persistent conversational thread — concept mining → proposal → revision — with the retrieved full-text passages in context throughout; (2) the Critic Panel acts as an automated peer-review step: two independent critic agents (the judges of the aggregation step), backed by distinct role backends (distinct providers when so configured), grade the entire candidate pool jointly in a single pass (rather than by pairwise tournament), with all judgments grounded in the candidates' cited passages; grades, mechanism-step and terminology audits, pool ranking, and the critique are produced in one single-pass call per judge, and a third judge is consulted only when the two judges disagree. Solid arrows denote control and object flow; dashed arrows denote audit records and optional paths.

Experiment design: after confirmation, each committed hypothesis passes to a two-role stage — the Experiment Designer expands the proposal's one-line experiment sketch into a full, retrieval-grounded plan (design · operationalization · baselines, datasets, and metrics · controls drawn from the graph's confounder and mediator nodes · expected versus falsifying outcomes) — the targeted retrieval that grounds the plan (methods, datasets, baselines) is an optional input to this stage — and the independent Experiment Validator, an automated reviewer, grades the plan on five criteria (clarity, validity, robustness, feasibility, reproducibility), driving a single revision pass. The validated plan is accepted into the graph as a structured Δ-experiment bound to its hypothesis (producing a new graph revision) and exported; the optional Narrative Elaborator writes reader-facing prose for each surfaced hypothesis at render time, including its committed plan when one exists. This stage requires no further researcher interaction.

2 · The same pipeline, in plain words

Read it top-to-bottom. The same stages and arrows as section 1, with every label in plain language, and one drawing difference: the Critic Panel — the only step that really runs several agents at once — is drawn here as its individual judges instead of one box.

You bring a claim or research goal, your expertise, and your topic priorities.

① Map your claim — the Extraction Agent turns the claim into a small graph of concepts joined by directed links, each recording a relationship a source asserts.

② Propose ⟷ critique — the Research Synthesist reads that graph together with full-text passages from the retrieved papers and drafts hypotheses; two independent critic agents each grade and rank them (a third judge joins only when the two disagree); the Research Synthesist revises against the critique. The best few are then shown to you, and no hypothesis is saved until you, or the confirmation policy in your profile, confirm it.

③ Check the evidence — the Evidence Reviewer reads every short-listed passage behind each link, and a fixed rule — not the reviewer — turns those readings into the verdict.

④ Design the experiments — for every hypothesis you confirmed, the Experiment Designer writes a full test plan and the independent Experiment Validator grades it, with one round of revision.

In the middle sits the claim graph, the one shared record: every result is saved into it, every stage after ① reads from it, and at the end it hands you your hypotheses with their experiment plans.
