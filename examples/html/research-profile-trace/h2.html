Research question: When does structured pruning reduce inference cost while preserving factual accuracy?

Proposed hypothesis h2

A factuality cliff may mark the useful pruning limit.

Possible explanation

What might be happening: Pruning may improve latency smoothly while factual accuracy stays stable only up to a threshold, then drops quickly.

Why it matters: A threshold curve would help choose compression ratios that save inference cost without silently sacrificing factuality.

How to check: Sweep pruning ratios across benchmarks and fit piecewise curves for latency and factual accuracy.

What would change our mind: The idea weakens if accuracy declines linearly with no detectable threshold.

Status: This is a testable idea, not a proven conclusion.

  • factuality cliff threshold — the point where factual accuracy starts falling faster than latency improves (Example profile)

What problem this solves

Latency gains are easy to measure, but the safe factuality boundary is usually unclear.
