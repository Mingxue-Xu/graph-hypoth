# Research Agenda: Claims, Current Implementation, and Proposed Studies

*Complete report. A four-page decision version is at [research-agenda-decision.md](research-agenda-decision.md).*

## Abstract

**System goal, in fifteen words:** Help researchers generate, evaluate, test, and revise scientific hypotheses using traceable evidence and calibrated uncertainty. This is the intended research goal, not a statement that the present system achieves every part of it. The shipped graph-state workflow implements extraction, literature appraisal, hypothesis development, confirmation and experiment-plan recording. Experimental execution, calibrated scientific judgments and improved researcher outcomes require further evidence or implementation.

This agenda turns the findings of [From Traceable Claims to Testable Hypotheses](traceable-claims-to-testable-hypotheses.md) into studies. It develops four studies in full because they form a chain through the deterministic core that the companion analysis identifies as the system's distinctive contribution: expose claims to counterevidence (A), calibrate what is accepted (B), revise conclusions when evidence changes, including evidence from executed experiments (C), and demonstrate that the resulting record helps a researcher (D). Three further claims are treated as foundations that must be built before A–D can be measured credibly, and the remaining claims and later directions are summarised with the reason each is deferred. All additions are research proposals inferred from literature and code inspection; none is presented as an improvement already measured in GraphHypothesize.

## 1. How the agenda was derived

The companion analysis establishes that GraphHypothesize's checks are structural: they control what enters the research record without establishing that the record is scientifically right. It lists seven studies that would connect process guarantees to measured research utility. A first version of this agenda ranked twelve claims by their relevance to the system goal, an editorial judgment about direct contribution to researcher benefit, the scientific consequence of a missing capability, and its reach across the workflow. Demonstrating researcher benefit ranked first as the criterion for the whole system; evidence-aware generation, discriminating tests, execution, revision, selection and calibrated uncertainty followed as the capabilities through which that benefit would be delivered; and challenge retrieval, meaning preservation, citation support, provenance and independent corroboration completed the list as enabling conditions whose lower position reflects their role, not permission to tolerate their absence.

Four claims are developed here. They were chosen because all three review lines converge on them, because each touches the deterministic core directly, and because they can be pursued inside the present representational choice. Claim A is the reachability gap: the cheapest fix with the largest effect on what the graph can say. Claim B asks whether the confidence number means anything, on which every downstream decision and the whole local-model question depend. Claim C closes the loop that is currently open at both ends, folding execution in as the main source of new evidence. Claim D tests the architectural thesis itself. Hypothesis selection (former claim 6) is the strongest candidate left out: it matters for output quality, but its literature concerns judge validity rather than this design, and it does not depend on the deterministic core.

**Conventions.** Each claim states the current public implementation, the literature and its limits, the addition to evaluate, and the evidence of progress that would count. "Current" refers to the shipped workflow and defaults; code links name the module, and the relevant functions are named in prose. Literature is cited by the primary record. The ranking and the choice of four are editorial, not results the literature supplies.

## 2. Study A: expose claims to counterevidence

**Current implementation.** The role vocabulary already includes support, contradiction, qualification, mechanism and confounder. The [verification cycle](../../src/cycles/verification.py) can appraise mixed-role evidence together and requires an appropriate role before committing each substantive verdict. However, the shipped workflow in [run_path.py](../../src/run_path.py) schedules support only, and the live dependency builder in [graph_state_runtime.py](../../src/graph_state_runtime.py) supplies the same claim-level evidence pool to every target, so selecting another role does not itself perform a new targeted challenge search. A computed adverse verdict can therefore lack the role needed for commitment and remain an open risk. [Claims Before Causes](claims-before-causes.md) calls this the reachability gap. The machinery exists; the schedule starves it.

**Literature and its limits.** SciFact separates evidence retrieval, stance and rationale selection, which makes retrieval failure and appraisal failure measurable apart <sup>[1](#reference-1)</sup>. OpenScholar supplies a scientific retrieval-and-synthesis comparator with retrieval ablations <sup>[2](#reference-2)</sup>. Yoon et al. identify a confound in generated query expansion: some apparent retrieval gains coincide with generated text already containing information supported by the reference evidence, a confound any generated challenge query inherits <sup>[3](#reference-3)</sup>. CONFACT motivates testing conflicting documents together with source metadata, though its media-credibility metadata concern neither statistical independence nor scientific risk of bias <sup>[4](#reference-4)</sup>. None establishes complete coverage of the literature relevant to a live hypothesis.

**Addition to evaluate.** Connect role scheduling to a bounded retrieval policy that actively searches for contradiction, qualification, causal alternatives and methodological challenges. Preserve the original claim, the generated queries, the returned passages and the sources that could not be accessed as separate records, with unexpanded retrieval as a comparator. Combine the relevant roles before commitment. Record that a challenge search found nothing without interpreting this as evidence that no counterevidence exists. Keep "no meaningful effect", "explicit denial of causation" and "inconclusive estimate" distinguishable, since these are different evidential situations even when they bear on the same claim. Group evidence by underlying study, because multiple articles or summaries based on one dataset must not automatically count as independent corroboration; shared authors or datasets are signals to investigate, not sufficient proof of dependence.

**Study design.** Build a curated evidence collection for a bounded set of claims, annotated for support, contradiction, scope restriction, methodological criticism and irrelevance, with study-level grouping. Compare the present shared-pool workflow with targeted challenge retrieval under the same initial claim, total query budget and reading budget; a separate comparison can test adaptive allocation against a fixed allocation. Hold the appraiser constant to isolate retrieval effects, and supply the same expert-curated evidence to every appraiser to isolate appraisal effects. Both are necessary because a correct verdict under a supplied-evidence condition, in which the curated counterevidence is handed to the appraiser directly, says little about whether the system would retrieve it. Audit generated queries separately, retaining original claims, generated queries and returned passages as distinct records.

**Evidence of progress.** Outcomes are relevant evidence recovered, coverage of annotated evidence roles, duplication by study, and verdict error after retrieval, with recall defined against the curated collection rather than all potentially available literature. Retain empty searches, abstentions and unsuccessful runs; an empty search is not a scientific null result, and a nonsignificant study result is not automatically evidence of no effect. The intervention fails its principal test if additional counterevidence queries merely increase document volume without improving role coverage or reducing unsupported acceptance at comparable coverage. If counterevidence is recovered but ignored, the result identifies an appraisal or commitment problem instead of a retrieval problem, and the work moves to Study B.

## 3. Study B: calibrate acceptance against an explicit target

**Current implementation.** Evidence appraisal produces recorded sub-signals; deterministic formulas in the [verification cycle](../../src/cycles/verification.py) combine support, counterevidence, breadth, risks and qualifiers, and the selector can return insufficient evidence when scores are low or close. These are meaningful controls over consistency and abstention. The current tests pin configured constants and behaviour; a test named "calibrated" in [test_graph_config_defaults.py](../../tests/unit/test_graph_config_defaults.py) does not itself estimate calibration against independent outcomes. No empirical calibration result was located in the public implementation and test material. The same fact bounds the local-model question: a backend change shifts score distributions, and fixed thresholds then move verdicts even when rankings remain similar, which [Which Agents Go Local?](which-agents-go-local.md) lists as score drift. Calibration is therefore a precondition for deployment decisions as well as a scientific requirement.

**Literature and its limits.** Trust or Escalate offers a relevant selective-evaluation design: calibrate when to accept a model judgment and when to abstain or escalate to a stronger judge or a human, with a statistical target of agreement with human preferences under stated calibration assumptions <sup>[5](#reference-5)</sup>. Its demonstrations do not establish scientific truth, do not make an arbitrary scientific score a probability of truth, and offer no unrestricted guarantee under domain shift.

**Addition to evaluate.** Define the target before fitting a calibration model. One defensible target is agreement with independent expert adjudication of whether a stated claim is supported by the supplied evidence, which differs from the probability that the claim is true in the world. Expert uncertainty, unresolved disagreement and insufficient evidence should remain visible rather than being silently recoded as incorrect answers. Evaluate the existing deterministic scores first as scores. If they are converted into probabilities, fit the mapping on development data, freeze the acceptance policy, and assess on a separate test set in which paper families and closely related claims remain in the same partition. Report cross-domain and later-evidence tests separately from tests drawn from the calibration distribution. Escalate difficult cases to an independent reviewer, recording the cost and the reasons for abstention. Keep scientific truth, expert agreement and source support as separate concepts.

**Study design.** Hold candidate judgments fixed while varying the acceptance policy: the existing threshold, empirically calibrated selection, and escalation. Compare policies at matched coverage and cost.

**Evidence of progress.** Report the error among accepted judgments together with the fraction accepted, a risk–coverage curve, out-of-domain degradation and the cost of escalation. Where a probability target is justified, probability calibration and a proper scoring rule can supplement these measures. A policy that accepts almost nothing may be reliable but of little research use. Repeating model judgments more often should not be assumed to solve the problem, because replication of a shared error can increase apparent certainty. A useful negative result would be failure to preserve reliability outside the calibration domain, or no improvement over a simpler threshold at matched coverage and cost; such a result would limit the scope of any confidence claim rather than invalidate the deterministic rule.

## 4. Study C: revise conclusions and their dependents, with execution feedback

**Current implementation.** An insufficient verdict can move to a substantive verdict. Supported, contradicted, qualified and not-causal states are otherwise terminal through the verification transition rules in [validator.py](../../src/validator.py). Experiment-plan commitment attaches a plan without changing the claim's verdict. Candidate revision after critique exists elsewhere in the workflow, but that is different from revising a settled evidence assessment after new observations. The graph-state [experiment stage](../../src/cycles/experiment.py) retrieves methods, designs and optionally refines plans, and commits them against hypothesis edges with distinct grounding states for cited methods, retrieved-but-irrelevant methods and no methods retrieved. It does not execute those proposals, store a scientific outcome as a dedicated experiment result, or feed that outcome back through settled-verdict reassessment. The loop is open at both ends: nothing revises a settled verdict, and nothing produces the observation that would warrant revising it. The existing distinction between a plan and a verified edge should be preserved.

**Literature and its limits.** DeReLab supplies formally specified confirming, defeating and irrelevant updates, including exceptions, and BayesBench distinguishes updating beliefs from using them in later predictions <sup>[6](#reference-6), [7](#reference-7)</sup>. These controlled settings do not determine how much weight a real scientific study deserves, and exact Bayesian reference trajectories require a specified likelihood. On execution, POPPER connects measurable implications to executed sequential falsification with Type-I error control <sup>[8](#reference-8)</sup>; ReplicatorBench evaluates replication workflows including non-replicable cases <sup>[9](#reference-9)</sup>; Robin and Co-Scientist report selected biomedical follow-up <sup>[10](#reference-10), [11](#reference-11)</sup>. These results are bounded by domains, expert involvement and statistical assumptions; they do not validate every generated proposal.

**Addition to evaluate.** Introduce a governed reassessment transaction that retains the prior verdict, the changed evidence, the rationale and the new assessment, and that distinguishes status revision, scope narrowing, source withdrawal and an unchanged conclusion. Record claim dependencies so that a correction flags the downstream hypotheses and plans built on the corrected premise. Permit warranted revision without allowing preferences or duplicate evidence to overwrite the record. Then add an execution interface and durable result records linking the tested claim, the final protocol, data and code versions, quality controls, observed estimates, uncertainty and interpretation, connected to the reassessment mechanism. Begin with approved, bounded computational experiments; laboratory work would require an execution partner and domain-specific controls. Any sequential-testing layer must address whether each generated implication is logically appropriate for the hypothesis, adaptive data reuse, repeated testing and selection across many hypotheses, and the scope of its error control: a guarantee for one testing sequence should not be presented as control over the entire discovery program.

**Study design.** Begin with evidence sequences for which the desired update is independently specified: genuine contradiction, a narrower population or condition, duplicated evidence, withdrawal of a supporting source, and new information irrelevant to the claim. Match confirming and disconfirming updates for relevance and evidential strength; otherwise a difference in revision rates could reflect the evidence rather than an asymmetric response to it. The current terminal-verdict behaviour is a descriptive baseline, but merely allowing changes would make improvement over it too easy to interpret. The stronger comparison is between an incremental procedure with preserved history and full recomputation from the same accumulated evidence, with a second contrast varying evidence order while preserving the final evidence set. For execution, compare artifact-only generation, one execution, and execution with bounded revision, using independent checks and hidden parameter variations rather than accepting a generated success message.

**Evidence of progress.** Measure whether each required revision occurs, whether unaffected claims remain stable, whether qualified conclusions replace overbroad ones, and whether downstream hypotheses and plans are reconsidered when their premises change; the record should retain the prior conclusion and its evidence rather than overwriting the past. Turn-by-turn assessment is needed because stable final accuracy can conceal earlier failures. For execution, prespecify separate denominators for proposals generated, plans selected, executions attempted, results interpretable and claims updated. A quality-controlled execution with adequate measurement sensitivity and a prespecified negative or null outcome is informative; a failed assay or software execution is not falsification of the scientific claim. Test whether the resulting updates agree with independent analysis before claiming an autonomous scientific feedback loop. A procedure that changes more verdicts but also changes conclusions after irrelevant or duplicate evidence would not meet the hypothesis.

## 5. Study D: demonstrate researcher benefit against an equally complete dossier

**Current implementation.** The workflow in [run_path.py](../../src/run_path.py) exports graph state, evidence assessments, surfaced hypotheses, experiment plans and audit material, and existing unit and fixture-based integration tests check the behaviour of those mechanisms. Those checks do not measure whether researchers reach better scientific decisions. No controlled researcher-utility result was located in the public code and test material.

**Literature and its limits.** DiscoveryBench assesses data-driven hypotheses, ScienceAgentBench scientific analysis tasks, TruthInsightBench evidence-linked artifacts, and K-Bench real scientific requests and delivered work <sup>[12](#reference-12), [13](#reference-13), [14](#reference-14), [15](#reference-15)</sup>. Their units and reference judgments differ, and recent model-graded benchmarks cannot supply an independent scientific validity label merely because their score aggregation is deterministic. PROV-AGENT demonstrates a provenance representation linking model invocations, prompts, responses, tools and workflow data; it motivates a richer audit record but does not establish that a graph improves investigators' decisions <sup>[16](#reference-16)</sup>.

**Addition to evaluate.** Build a domain-stratified evaluation set with qualified source claims, relevant challenges, candidate pools and selected executable tasks. Follow component tests with a researcher study comparing three conditions: a final report alone, an equally complete flat evidence dossier, and the dossier plus the claim graph and its revision history. The dossier–graph contrast isolates the organisation of evidence from the benefit of receiving more of it. Match model access, reading time and tool budgets where applicable; counterbalance task order; and avoid assigning an investigator the same underlying problem twice when carryover would reveal the answer.

**Study design.** Tasks should require locating support for a claim, identifying a scope mismatch, distinguishing independent studies from repeated reports, explaining a revision, and determining which downstream proposals an evidential correction affects. Prespecified, deliberately introduced errors can test diagnostic sensitivity, but results from such probes should remain separate from the prevalence of naturally occurring errors. Reconstruction needs distinct tests: replaying preserved transactions to recover a state is different from rerunning model calls, from computationally reproducing an analysis with the original code and data, and from replicating a scientific result with new data; merges, rejected updates, source corrections and revised identifiers should be included because the usefulness of a provenance record depends on continuity through change. A pilot should refine annotation and estimate variability, after which sample size and analysis choices can be justified from the intended effect and available resources.

**Evidence of progress.** Primary outcomes are independently adjudicated task answers, correction of overbroad claims, identification of dependent evidence, quality of proposed tests, error localisation and investigator time; perceived usefulness and clarity are secondary. Report disagreement, abstention, failed runs, expert disagreement, cost and all prespecified exclusions, with uncertainty at the paper or research-problem level. The strongest support for the architecture would be an improvement over the equally complete dossier in warranted decisions or efficient correction, sustained when evidence changes. If that contrast shows no benefit, traceability may still be useful, but the claim that graph organisation improves research reasoning would remain unproven.

## 6. Foundations

Three lower-ranked claims are prerequisites for measuring A–D credibly. They are engineering work with a reasonably clear specification rather than open research questions, and they come first in the sequence.

**Meaning preservation.** Nodes have definitions, aliases and scope qualifiers, and edges have relationship text, conditions and confounders, but initial extraction commits through structural and reference checks alone; the scope-gap calculation runs after commitment when the extractor supplies facets, and canonicalization is available only for mined concepts during enrichment. Neither a free-form relationship label nor an existing textual condition guarantees preservation of polarity, modality or study context. Claimify separates entailment, coverage and decontextualisation; EDC separates extraction from relation definition and canonicalization; Hagen et al. distinguish denial of causation from negative causal effects; and Lu et al. show why finer decomposition is not automatically better for a verifier <sup>[17](#reference-17), [18](#reference-18), [19](#reference-19), [20](#reference-20)</sup>. The addition is a source-aligned claim record with explicit polarity, modality, relation direction and relevant scope, permitting unknown or inapplicable fields, compared with its source before commitment for both altered meaning and missing content, with contextual canonicalization applied consistently and the original passage retained. Progress is measured by comparing current extraction with semantic checking and with semantic checking plus canonicalization on the same passages: meaning-changing errors, omissions, false merges and downstream appraisal. A cleaner graph that omits difficult claims or merges scientifically distinct claims would not establish a benefit.

**Citation warrant.** Retrieval includes quote matching with exact, normalised and approximate sentence-window outcomes, and the Evidence Reviewer makes model-based entailment and context judgments, but experiment-plan checks resolve declared evidence identifiers within the retrieved batch and require nonempty quotations without establishing that every plan-authored quotation matches its source or that the passage supports the proposed method. CLAIM-BENCH evaluates claims, evidence and their links in full papers, with lower agreement on evidence annotations than on claims, and SourceCheckup examines whether medical-answer statements are supported by their cited sources <sup>[21](#reference-21), [22](#reference-22)</sup>. The addition is one citation audit applied at extraction, hypothesis commitment and final plan review: resolve the batch and source, check quotation fidelity, and assess whether the passage warrants the attached assertion within scope, keeping exact quotation, normalised match, approximate match and paraphrase distinct and distinguishing a source that supports an established method from one supposedly validating its novel application. An openly ungrounded proposal can remain available with its evidential status visible. Progress is measured with blinded expert judgments of claim–passage and method–passage pairs, with unsupported attribution and false rejection of legitimate support reported separately.

**Reconstructable provenance.** Transactions retain accepted and rejected proposals with deltas, and a replay method reapplies accepted deltas from an empty store, but ordinary exports contain the graph, tables, memo and receipt summaries rather than the complete transaction rows that replay requires; current event writes use no predecessor hash; and node merging recomputes edge identifiers without remapping linked evidence and plans. PROV-AGENT supplies a representational precedent, not evidence of completeness or tamper resistance <sup>[16](#reference-16)</sup>. The addition is to export full transaction payloads with the initial state and the source and configuration references needed to reconstruct them, preserve source versions and stable claim identities across merges with an explicit mapping for retired identifiers, connect retrieval, appraisal, candidate derivation, final plans and results in one provenance record, and, if alteration detection is an objective, specify and test a hash chain or equivalent integrity mechanism separately. Progress is measured by asking an independent investigator to reconstruct selected decisions, locate their evidence and explain changes using only an exported run, including merges, rejected changes and source withdrawal.

## 7. Deferred claims

The claims below were part of the twelve, or among the directions proposed afterwards. Each is deferred because it depends on A–D, because its literature concerns judge validity rather than this design, or because it broadens scope beyond the system. One paragraph each records the current state, the anchor literature and the addition, so that none is lost.

**Evidence-aware generation.** The Research Synthesist mines concepts, receives literature passages, proposes mechanisms and source quotations, and repairs or derives candidates after panel feedback, but its committed-graph rendering omits recorded edge verdicts and confidence, and candidate parsing does not reject every candidate lacking a resolved new edge while the experiment stage skips candidates with none to bind. SciMON evaluates literature- and graph-informed idea generation while identifying shallow recombination, and Si et al. report promising expert novelty ratings alongside limitations in diversity and automatic ranking <sup>[23](#reference-23), [24](#reference-24)</sup>. The addition is to supply the generator with source-linked premise assessments, unresolved counterevidence and scope qualifications, marking which premises are accepted, uncertain or deliberately challenged, and to record a hypothesis as a testable relation, mechanism or boundary condition. Compare graph context with and without premise assessments at fixed literature, model and budget; a gain in caution that removes valuable falsification or replication questions would undermine the goal. Feeds A and C.

**Discriminating tests.** Plans already contain operationalisation, baselines, controls, metrics, expected outcomes, falsification, feasibility and grounding, and the designer can receive graph-derived confounders and targeted methods passages; but the procedure field is not required, review can be disabled, and the last refinement need not receive another model review. BioPlanner evaluates structured protocol planning and BED-LLM uses information objectives for question selection; neither establishes the discriminating value of scientific plans <sup>[25](#reference-25), [26](#reference-26)</sup>. The addition is to require plausible alternative explanations, predicted observations under each, a discriminating measurement and interpretation rules for positive, negative, mixed and failed-execution outcomes, and to make procedure detail and final review explicit requirements for an execution-ready plan. Report estimated information gain separately from realised discrimination. Feeds C.

**Valuable selection.** Critic inputs hide the generator's rationale and self-scores; the panel grades novelty and saturation, audits mechanisms and terminology and produces listwise rankings; aggregation uses medians and rank fusion; and final surfacing uses hypothesis scores with saturation demotion. RINoBench and RQ-Bench question automatic novelty judgments, PoLL supplies positive panel evidence, the Geometry of LLM-as-Judge shows shared judgments departing from human ratings, and HindSight offers later-publication matching with a training-cutoff confound <sup>[27](#reference-27), [28](#reference-28), [29](#reference-29), [30](#reference-30), [31](#reference-31)</sup>. The addition is to retain candidate pools and selection histories, evaluate novelty, plausibility, testability, feasibility, replication value and boundary-condition value separately, audit a random sample of judge agreements, counterbalance order, compare heterogeneous panels at matched cost, and test whether saturation demotion discards useful replications. Feeds D.

**Independent corroboration.** The retrieval ledger deduplicates paper records and the Evidence Reviewer grades methodological properties, but the confidence breadth term counts distinct source identifiers and nothing establishes whether papers reuse the same participants, dataset or experimental series. The Cochrane Handbook distinguishes studies from reports, and Hedges, Tipton and Johnson address dependent effect estimates <sup>[32](#reference-32), [33](#reference-33)</sup>; statistical treatment of dependent numerical estimates cannot be applied unchanged to free-text judgments. The addition is a distinction among document, study and evidence observation, recording dataset or cohort identifiers, conditions, primary versus secondary reporting, reanalysis, replication and known overlap, with unknown dependence preserved explicitly, before any dependence-aware contribution to confidence is tested. Feeds B.

**Verified-outcome routing.** A cheaper call is not necessarily a cheaper completed task once retries, corrections, review and correlated mistakes are counted. RouteLLM learns routing from preference data, learning to defer models dependence between AI and human predictions, and active data selection frames acquisition around the information sought <sup>[34](#reference-34), [35](#reference-35), [36](#reference-36)</sup>; none establishes that preference-trained routing preserves scientific validity. Roles already accept individual model configurations, which enables controlled substitutions but is not an adaptive router. Start with paired replay of the same role inputs through candidate backends, fit routing rules on separate tasks, and compare fixed larger, fixed smaller, role-based and learned routing on held-out tasks, reporting cost per verified completion together with completion rate so a policy cannot look efficient by failing difficult tasks. Feeds B and the local-model roadmap.

**Joint retrieval and reader evaluation.** A retriever can locate the right material while the reader fails to use it, loses a qualifier or cannot act within its tool budget. Sen et al. show that scores depend on the harness and delivery style as well as the search method, and ALCE evaluates retrieval followed by cited generation <sup>[37](#reference-37), [38](#reference-38)</sup>. The retrieval service already combines configured sources, evidence processing and optional reranking; the addition is a controlled harness crossing lexical, dense and hybrid retrieval with candidate readers and inline versus separate-read delivery, with oracle-, missing-, misleading- and irrelevant-evidence conditions. A retriever ranking that reverses across readers is evidence against a universal ranking, not a failed experiment. Feeds A and [Which Agents Go Local?](which-agents-go-local.md).

**Communication fidelity.** Translation, simplification and analogy can preserve the topic while changing what is claimed. Hayakawa, Bott and Saggion report that automatic-metric gains in small-model simplification can accompany more harmful substitutions, and xCOMET combines sentence-level evaluation with localised error detection <sup>[39](#reference-39), [40](#reference-40)</sup>. The reader translator already proposes term mappings, obtains a separate verifier judgment, retries rejected mappings and applies deterministic guards, but this does not establish preservation of every factual or hedged statement. Compare original text, unconstrained rewriting, the current term-level process and whole-assertion fidelity checking, annotating polarity, uncertainty, population, conditions, units and citation links before and after rewriting; no automatic metric should serve as its own ground truth. Feeds D.

A further direction, distinguishing reusable evidence from permission to make a stronger causal inference, is developed in [Claims Before Causes](claims-before-causes.md) under the conditions for promoting a claim to a cause.

## 8. The same problems in other domains

The four claims are not specific to science. A debugging assistant, a policy-document analyst and a decision-support tool all derive decisions from evidence: what to propose, which alternative to examine, when to act, and what to revise after an observation. The earlier cross-domain agenda broadened each of the twelve claims with a methodological precedent from another field. Four of those precedents bear directly on A–D and are worth borrowing.

For researcher benefit (D), controlled human–AI interaction studies manipulate advice correctness and explanation correctness and separate speed, appropriate reliance and decision quality <sup>[41](#reference-41), [42](#reference-42)</sup>; the researcher study above can be extended into a randomised interaction study with assistance before and after an initial judgment, including a second task such as identifying causes of a software failure. For revision (C), truth maintenance records assumptions and justifications and incremental view maintenance propagates changes through derived results <sup>[46](#reference-46), [47](#reference-47)</sup>; typed dependencies for evidence, assumptions, transformations and decision policies would let an update mark affected outputs as unchanged, stale, revised or invalidated. For calibration (B), selective prediction and conformal risk control address a defined expected loss under stated exchangeability assumptions, and ASPEST combines selective prediction with active labelling under shift <sup>[49](#reference-49), [50](#reference-50)</sup>; conformal guarantees apply only where a specific result covers the chosen loss and data assumptions. For challenge retrieval (A), relevance–diversity ranking offers a baseline for reducing redundant retrieval, and active learning distinguishes uncertainty that further observations may resolve from ambiguity that additional samples need not remove <sup>[48](#reference-48), [51](#reference-51)</sup>. Precedents for the deferred claims include procedure-grounded test generation and program testing for evidence-aware generation and discriminating tests <sup>[43](#reference-43), [44](#reference-44)</sup>, execution feedback in code generation for execution <sup>[45](#reference-45)</sup>, Abstract Meaning Representation for meaning preservation <sup>[52](#reference-52)</sup>, and PROV-DM for interoperable provenance <sup>[53](#reference-53)</sup>. Transfer in either direction is a hypothesis; the scientific workflow is not already a general decision-support platform, and every non-scientific task requires suitable adapters and reference judgments.

## 9. Implementation sequence and work order

The sequence below concerns implementation dependencies, separately from the importance ranking. A lower-ranked enabling capability may need to be built first, and evaluation of researcher benefit should shape the work from the outset.

| Stage | Existing capability to retain | Addition to evaluate | Why it comes at this stage |
|---|---|---|---|
| First: trustworthy inputs and records | Structural gates, evidence roles, paper deduplication, quote matching, transaction records | Semantic admission, citation audits, complete exports and merge continuity (foundations); targeted challenge retrieval and study grouping (A) | Later judgments need claims, evidence and histories that can be independently checked |
| Next: warranted judgments | Deterministic scoring, abstention, critic blinding, candidate revision | Held-out calibration and escalation (B); governed reassessment (C, first half) | These test whether existing controls improve scientific judgment |
| Then: informative testing | Graph-informed methods retrieval and structured plans | Bounded execution, outcome records and result-driven revision (C, second half); explicit alternatives and final-plan review (deferred) | Execution should be attached to an inspectable claim and interpretation rule |
| Throughout, with a final prospective study | Existing fixture and integration checks | Measurement design, failure accounting and comparison with a complete evidence dossier (D) | Evaluation criteria must be fixed before interpreting apparent gains |

A comes first among the studies because it is cheapest and because its evaluation produces the adjudicated cases B needs; C needs B's acceptance policy to decide when a revision is warranted; D is last because it tests the record the others produce. The smallest coherent extension would connect source-aligned claims, deliberate challenge retrieval, inspectable reassessment and complete provenance, then test that chain on a bounded set of independently adjudicated cases. Broader autonomy should be justified by the resulting evidence. For each study, preregister the primary outcome and acceptable trade-offs before evaluating held-out tasks, and report failures, uncompleted cases and human effort. Separate improvements to an individual component from improvements to the full decision process. The goal does not require maximising graph size, novelty scores, the number of agents or the number of committed plans.

## 10. Scope and limitations

This is a selective, literature-informed agenda, not a systematic review or a demonstration of new system performance. The literature review was question-directed, using primary papers and relevant methods and results passages; the claim comparisons rechecked the graph-state implementation and selected primary records but did not reproduce the papers' experiments, run a new scientific study, or implement the additions. Missing empirical validation means none was located within the public code and research material, not that unpublished evidence cannot exist. The importance ranking and the selection of four claims are editorial judgments; the literature does not establish either ordering. Publication status distinguishes a venue record from author-reported acceptance, and a preprint label means no later venue was verified.
